# Product and Commercial Validation

## Three distinct validations
1. **Partnership:** Does an organisation want to collaborate?
2. **User:** Can a target user complete a meaningful task and receive value?
3. **Commercial:** Is the problem important enough that someone will pay to solve it?

One does not prove another.

## Build one observable workflow
For ScoutSA, the proposed first slice is a tournament organiser workflow:
- Register a tournament.
- Manage basic details and participating teams.
- Record results and relevant statistics.
- Associate players and coaches through stable identifiers.
- Correct mistakes without corrupting records.
- Display what is verified and unverified.

This is a proposed milestone, not a claim that the workflow currently exists.

## Usability test record
Record participant type (avoid unnecessary personal data), task attempted, completion, friction, errors, expected versus actual behaviour, assistance required, willingness to reuse and evidence/consent to record feedback.

Do not convert friendly praise into proof of demand. Repeated use, task completion and observed behaviour are stronger signals.

## Commercial evidence ladder
| Stage | Evidence | Do not claim |
|---|---|---|
| Problem hypothesis | Interviews, research, observed pain | Validated demand |
| Usability | Target users complete tasks | Product-market fit |
| Repeat use | Users return and complete workflows | Willingness to pay |
| Payment intent | Specific price discussion or credible commitment | Revenue |
| Transaction | Actual payment or contract | Scalable business |
| Retention / expansion | Renewal, repeat purchase or sustained use | Guaranteed future growth |

## Weekly scorecard
Track actual numbers and evidence links: working workflows; critical tests passed/total; open defects by severity; user tasks attempted/completed; repeat users; willingness-to-pay evidence; transactions; documentation versus implementation/validation time; unresolved assumptions and risks.

Do not invent targets before a baseline exists. State denominator, time period and source for every metric.
