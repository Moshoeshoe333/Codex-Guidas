# Opportunity Exposure Field Note
## The Luck Field Manual — 33 heuristics across nine areas

**Record ID:** OFN-001  
**Recorded:** 2026-11-24  
**Record type:** External framework / strategic-practice note  
**Status:** Observed source; derived applications are hypotheses, not verified controls  
**Authority:** Informational only. This note does not change any project gate, implementation decision, safeguarding rule or release authority.

## 1. Why this is recorded

A post shared by [@AlwaysKeepL on X](https://x.com/AlwaysKeepL/status/2125001458856693838) pointed to a short manual about systematically widening one's exposure to opportunity. The underlying work is attributed to **Polymath Investor**, dated **2026-10-05**. The originating two-post thread is available through [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2107085142204490025.html). A separate [transcribed reference copy, *The Luck Field Manual*](https://www.kwharrison13.com/notes/the-luck-field-manual/) records the six-page, 33-rule structure and its nine sections.

The underlying idea is to enlarge the number and quality of points at which a person or project can encounter opportunity, while developing the ability to recognise useful openings and remain resilient enough to act on them. The manual uses the phrase "luck surface area" for this contact zone.

**Provenance caution:** the X post supplied to us is a resharing account, not the author of the manual. The attribution and date above are taken from the independent archive and thread record. The six-page manual is reconstructed from images in the thread; the archive notes that the original was not published as a PDF.

## 2. The nine areas and 33 paraphrased heuristics

The short descriptions below are paraphrases for internal use, not a verbatim reproduction of the source.

### I. Increase the number of meaningful attempts (4)
1. Prefer repeated, thoughtful attempts over waiting indefinitely for a perfect moment.
2. Run small experiments quickly and stop approaches that do not work.
3. Take action because action can create options that planning alone cannot.
4. Increase the rate of useful encounters with people, problems and environments.

### II. Make credible work discoverable (3)
5. Do worthwhile work and communicate what was done.
6. Share learning in public where doing so is safe and useful.
7. Maintain a clear, credible and findable professional identity online.

### III. Build bridges between people and communities (5)
8. Recognise that acquaintances and looser connections can expose us to information beyond our close circle.
9. Connect groups or disciplines that rarely interact.
10. Be willing to initiate respectful conversations with unfamiliar people.
11. Make useful introductions and help others connect.
12. Preserve trust and avoid needlessly damaging relationships.

### IV. Improve positioning (3)
13. Spend time in places and forums where relevant opportunities actually gather.
14. Participate consistently in a useful professional or community ecosystem.
15. Develop a distinctive combination of skills, knowledge and experience.

### V. Protect the downside (4)
16. Look for opportunities where the potential upside is meaningfully larger than the bounded cost.
17. Avoid bets that could cause total or unrecoverable loss.
18. Balance safe, resilient foundations with a limited number of higher-upside experiments.
19. Remove avoidable fragility before expanding exposure.

### VI. Preserve flexibility (4)
20. Keep spare time, resources and capacity rather than operating permanently at the limit.
21. Make reversible decisions promptly while treating irreversible decisions with greater care.
22. Use short commitments where uncertainty is high.
23. Adapt when new evidence changes the situation.

### VII. Improve opportunity recognition (4)
24. Leave enough attentional space to notice unexpected signals.
25. Break repetitive routines to encounter different information and perspectives.
26. Build real depth in at least one useful area.
27. Stay open to unexpected combinations and connections without confusing curiosity with proof.

### VIII. Learn from adverse conditions (2)
28. Search for usable learning or a changed route after a setback without denying the real cost.
29. Treat interactions and delivered work as opportunities to demonstrate reliability and judgment.

### IX. Compound over time (4)
30. Maintain a constructive orientation toward future possibilities without assuming success is guaranteed.
31. Invest in durable relationships built through reciprocal value and trust.
32. Continue adding complementary skills.
33. Persist intelligently, revising methods when evidence shows that the current approach is failing.

## 3. Internal interpretation

The framework can be usefully represented as:

**Exposure to relevant environments + credible positioning + ability to recognise an opening + resilience to act = a larger opportunity surface.**

This is a conceptual model, not a measured equation or a promise that behaviour controls luck. More exposure can increase the number of chances to encounter opportunity, but it cannot guarantee access, fairness, selection, funding, employment or success.

## 5. Guardrails and counterweights

- **Exposure is not access.** Being visible does not mean a decision-maker can or will offer access.
- **Visibility is not trust.** Public activity must be supported by reliable work, honest claims and safe conduct.
- **Opportunity is not outcome.** An introduction, application, demo or invitation is not a placement, contract, investment or adoption.
- **Volume is not a licence for careless quality.** Repeated attempts should be bounded, ethical and informative.
- **Weak ties are not universally superior.** Strong relationships, expertise, credentials and trusted referrals may matter more in specific contexts.
- **Public visibility has costs.** Privacy, cybersecurity, reputation, safeguarding and personal safety must be assessed before publishing or networking.
- **Persistence is not sunk-cost escalation.** Stop or change an approach when evidence, risk or opportunity cost warrants it.
- **Asymmetric bets still need downside limits.** Do not risk essential living needs, safety, legal compliance or project integrity for speculative upside.

Retain the relevant portfolio invariants: **Exposure ≠ Opportunity; Visibility ≠ Access; Discoverability ≠ Trust; Contact ≠ Outcome.**

## 7. Evidence quality and current decision

The transcribed archive itself characterises the manual as a practitioner's synthesis rather than research, and describes its advice as directional rules of thumb rather than rigid mandates. We therefore classify it as an **external heuristic / source-backed summary**, not an empirically validated causal model.

**Decision:** Preserve and selectively test the ideas. No automatic adoption. Any proposed application must have a bounded hypothesis, explicit risks, a low-cost test, measurable success and failure criteria, and a review date. A successful local experiment supports only the tested context; it does not validate all 33 heuristics.
