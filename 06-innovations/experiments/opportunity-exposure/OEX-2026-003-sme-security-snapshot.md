# OEX-2026-003 — SME Security Snapshot Problem Discovery

**Recorded:** 2026-11-24  
**Status:** PRE-REGISTERED · DESK-RESEARCH ONLY · NO SELLING OR OUTREACH  
**Portfolio context:** UCI concept exploration  
**Related framework:** OFN-001 / CG-042

## 1. Candidate concept

An affordable, clearly bounded security-posture and third-party/operator assurance snapshot for small and medium-sized organisations, potentially followed by a lightweight continuous-assurance service.

This is an existing concept direction in the UCI opportunity set. It is **not** a selected product, approved service, legal-compliance product, security certification or statement that SMEs have already demonstrated demand.

## 2. Question and hypothesis

**Question:** Is there a recurring, sufficiently important SME problem around understanding basic cyber-security posture and third-party/operator exposure that a small, transparent and affordable assessment could address without promising more than it can evidence?

**Hypothesis:** Some SMEs may lack a low-cost, decision-useful way to understand selected security and supplier risks. A narrowly scoped evidence-based snapshot may help if it is more understandable, affordable and actionable than current alternatives.

The hypothesis can be false. The test must be allowed to conclude that the need is weak, already served, too expensive to deliver responsibly, or too risky for our current capability.

## 3. Scope and timebox

**Proposed window:** 2026-11-24 to 2026-12-08 (14 calendar days), desk research only.

**Allowed now:**
- Review public primary and authoritative sources about relevant SME cyber-risk needs, supplier/third-party exposure, and applicable South African obligations.
- Map existing products, providers, alternatives and publicly disclosed pricing where available.
- Examine public procurement, accelerator, ESD, insurer or industry channels as possible future routes; record eligibility as unknown until verified.
- Draft a one-page problem definition and candidate service boundary for internal review.

**Not allowed under this pre-registration:**
- No unsolicited calls, emails, interviews, sales pitches, paid placements or service commitments.
- No collection or handling of SME systems data, credentials, personal information, incident logs or confidential supplier records.
- No representation that the service assures POPIA compliance, prevents breaches, certifies suppliers or provides comprehensive security.
- No paid software, advertising, incorporation or other financial commitment.

## 4. Independent research lanes

| Lane | Questions | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| Need / risk | Which recurring security and third-party risks are actually documented for smaller organisations? Which are material to their operations? | South African government/regulator publications, incident/advisory data with scope limitations, credible industry research and primary source material |
| Existing alternatives | What can SMEs already buy or access, at what stated price and scope? Where do managed service providers, consultants, insurers, platforms or free resources overlap? | Provider service pages, scope descriptions, public prices, tender/procurement records and direct product documentation |
| Buyer / distribution | Who owns the problem and budget? What event triggers purchase? Could an SME use existing MSP, accounting, insurance, industry or ESD relationships? | Public buyer-role evidence, published programme eligibility, procurement material and clearly labelled hypotheses where direct evidence is absent |

Keep the lanes independent where possible. Five syndicated articles repeating one upstream claim count as one source family, not five independent signals.

## 5. Candidate product boundary to challenge

Possible deliverable under consideration only:

- A scoped inventory of evidence reviewed and not reviewed.
- A plain-language snapshot of selected controls and supplier-risk signals.
- Explicit findings, evidence links, confidence, gaps and time-bounded observations.
- A prioritised action list with owners and suggested next checks.
- A clear statement of exclusions, assumptions and residual risks.

Do not include intrusive testing, access to client systems, penetration testing, legal opinions, certification, identity verification or guarantees in the initial concept. Those capabilities would require distinct competence, authority, insurance, legal review and operational controls.

## 6. Success, failure and stop criteria

**Continue to a separately approved adult-buyer discovery phase only if:**
1. At least three independent, credible source signals support a recurring and specific SME problem (not merely general concern about cyber risk).
2. A plausible buyer/decision owner and purchase trigger can be identified.
3. Existing alternatives and a credible differentiation hypothesis are documented.
4. A deliverable can be defined within current competence without overstating regulatory, privacy or security assurance.
5. The delivery cost, liability, data-handling needs and downside appear bounded enough to justify further investigation.

**Change or stop if:**
- The problem is already adequately served by accessible providers or public resources.
- No buyer or budget owner can be identified.
- SMEs need a level of assurance, technical access or legal advice we cannot safely provide.
- Delivery economics are implausible at a genuinely affordable price.
- Research depends on unsupported market-size claims, unverified statistics or a false promise of compliance.

These are proposed decision thresholds for this small desk test, not universal standards.

## 7. Measurement plan

Record each relevant source with: claim ID; source owner; exact publication date; event date where different; access date; original URL; source type; independence family; passage or concise note; what it supports; what it does not support; contradictions; confidence; and next verification.

Track separately:
- recurring problem signals;
- identified buyer roles;
- purchase triggers;
- available alternatives;
- published price/scope evidence;
- delivery cost and capability gaps;
- unresolved legal, privacy, security and liability questions.

Do not count likes, views, directory listings, general awareness statements or generic partner enthusiasm as willingness to pay.

## 8. Future exposure phase — requires separate approval

Only after desk research supports a clear, honest service boundary may we prepare a small, consent-led adult-buyer discovery exercise. That later phase must use a reviewed question guide, no sensitive operational data, no compliance promises, and no unpaid delivery of bespoke security work. Any live testing or client-data access requires separately documented authority and safety controls.
