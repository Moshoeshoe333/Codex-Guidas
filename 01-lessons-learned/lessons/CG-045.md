# CG-045 — Synthetic eligibility is not human authorization or a real-world outcome

- **Status:** Derived
- **Date discovered:** 2026-11-25
- **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-25
- **Source project:** ScoutSA
- **Source artifact/path:** `prototype/README.md`, sections “Review engine and invitation gate” and “Explicit limitations”
- **Commit / CI run / issue:** Exact head `125c48082633ec4bb744f5734b78cc659441e8ba`
- **Severity and rationale:** High — confusing a synthetic eligibility result with a human decision can create false claims of consent, approval, selection, invitation or safeguarding.
- **Applicability:** General; especially safeguarding, identity, eligibility and opportunity systems

## Event
The ScoutSA prototype README states that a successful synthetic result can reach only eligibility for separate human review. It is not a human decision. The evaluator returns `disclosurePermitted: false`; synthetic records do not establish real identity, age, guardian authority, consent, reviewer independence, evidence authenticity, organization authority or safeguarding status.

## Expected behaviour
A system should expose narrow, typed outcomes and preserve the boundary between automated screening, eligibility for review, authorized human decisions, disclosure and real-world outcomes.

## Actual behaviour
The prototype explicitly constrains its synthetic evaluator to eligibility for separate human review and denies disclosure. Its README also lists the real-world facts that the synthetic fixtures do not establish.

## Impact
A synthetic or automated score can be misrepresented as approval, readiness, invitation, placement or safety. In youth-facing systems this can create material safeguarding and trust risks.

## Root cause
A category error: technical evaluation of synthetic inputs is not proof of real-world identity, authority, consent or decision-making.

## Corrective action
Keep evaluator output narrowly scoped. Require separately evidenced human authority and project-governed decision steps before any real disclosure or action.

## Preventive / detective control
Use explicit outcome types and user-facing wording that distinguish “eligible for human review” from “approved”, “invited”, “trial-ready” or “placed”. Add negative tests proving that synthetic success cannot enable disclosure or contact.

## Regression verification
- Test or procedure: Inspect evaluator contract and synthetic invitation-review tests
- Command/environment: Source/document review; no production identity or authority verification is claimed
- Expected result: Synthetic success never authorizes disclosure or claims a real-world decision
- Actual result: README records `disclosurePermitted: false` and the limited review-eligibility outcome
- Evidence link: https://github.com/Moshoeshoe333/ScoutSA/blob/prototype/internal-demo/prototype/README.md

## Residual risk and limitations
This lesson records a design boundary, not proof that a real safeguarding service or legally compliant authorization system exists. The audit-history model is experimental, in-memory and not tamper-evident.
