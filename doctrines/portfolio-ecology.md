# Sustainable Portfolio Ecology

**Status:** Adopted principle; the numeric allocation below is a provisional planning hypothesis, not measured actual time or a permanent mandate.

## 1. Survival enables creation

Creation requires sustenance. Food, shelter, health, reliable income, rest and available working capacity are real engineering constraints, not distractions from the work.

A portfolio that consumes all available time on speculative complexity is not sustainable. We must protect the ability to live, learn, earn and continue creating.

## 2. Provisional 80/10/5/5 attention hypothesis

The proposed allocation is:

| Allocation | Workstream | Intended boundary |
|---:|---|---|
| 80% | ScoutSA — execution | Primary moving workflow and the smallest evidence-producing product milestone |
| 10% | UCI — intelligence | Bounded research and threat analysis; no unproven platform expansion |
| 5% | SoulSync — maintenance | Preserve and maintain existing artifacts; expand only against a justified need |
| 5% | NAVIGRE — preservation | Archive strategic insights and preserve the option to resume later |

This allocation is a starting hypothesis, not a timesheet, forecast, promise or proof of priority correctness. It must not override urgent livelihood needs, security incidents, project gates or actual capacity. If ScoutSA is blocked on external validation, do not fill the time with speculative architecture merely to maintain an 80% target.

Review allocation weekly against available hours, urgent obligations, project status, user evidence, risks and the value of the next action. Record deviations and reasons without guilt or invented precision.

## 3. Anti-fragmentation law

**Do not start a new project to escape the discipline required to finish, validate, pause or deliberately retire an existing one.**

Before creating a new project, answer:
- What concrete problem does it solve?
- Why can no existing project or small experiment address it?
- What evidence supports demand or strategic value?
- What will be stopped, paused or deprioritized to make room?
- What is the smallest reversible test?
- What is the explicit stop condition?

A new idea can be captured in the innovation register without becoming a new repository or product.

## 4. Focus and work-in-progress limits

- Maintain one primary execution milestone at a time.
- Keep other projects in explicit maintenance, research, preservation or paused states.
- Limit simultaneous unfinished work; finish or consciously stop before opening another major stream.
- Prefer a complete vertical slice and real feedback over multiplying documents, services or repositories.
- Do not mistake activity, commits, architecture volume or dashboards for customer value.

## 5. Sustainable operating rhythm

At each weekly review:
1. protect essential livelihood and recovery time;
2. name the one primary outcome;
3. identify the smallest next action that produces evidence or value;
4. list blockers and external dependencies;
5. set a work-in-progress limit;
6. decide what will not be worked on;
7. update the allocation only when facts justify it.

## 6. Stop and pause are valid decisions

Pausing a project is not failure when it preserves scarce resources and prevents unsafe or wasteful work. Record why it is paused, what would justify resuming it and how to preserve its assets. Retiring an idea after evidence disproves it is a successful learning outcome.
