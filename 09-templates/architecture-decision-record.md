> An ADR preserves the reasoning and consequences of a decision. It does not authorize implementation unless the named project authority accepts it.

## 1. Decision statement

State one specific decision in a sentence or two. Avoid bundling unrelated choices into one ADR.

## 2. Context and problem

- What has happened or changed?
- What problem, risk or opportunity requires a decision?
- Which users, systems, data or teams are affected?
- Why must this be decided now?
- What happens if no change is made?

## 3. Decision drivers and constraints

List the criteria that matter, such as:
- correctness, security, privacy and safeguarding;
- maintainability, team capability and handover;
- performance, reliability and recovery;
- interoperability and migration;
- accessibility and operating context;
- development and lifecycle cost;
- reversibility, vendor/dependency risk and support burden;
- legal, contractual and regulatory requirements.

Record hard constraints separately from preferences. Identify non-compensable requirements; do not average away a critical safety or integrity failure.
